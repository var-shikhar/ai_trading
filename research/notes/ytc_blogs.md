# YTC Blog posts (Lance Beggs): notes for hourly-to-daily swing trading

These are my paraphrased notes, not quotes. Page numbers are **PDF pages** of the "YTC Blog – Posts 1 to 100" PDF (309 pages). Read alongside `notes/ytc.md` (YTC Price Action Trader vols 2–3). These notes don't repeat that file; they point back to it where a blog post only restates the book.

## 1. What the collection is

- The PDF is really **two ebooks joined together**:
  - **Posts 1–100** (PDF p1–208). The dates run from 2 May 2011 to 13 Dec 2011. It opens with two "lesson in every session" articles (p10–20), which are then split up and reposted as individual posts (p21–34).
  - **Posts 101–150** (PDF p209–309). The dates run from 15 Dec 2011 to 23 May 2012. This book's own page numbers equal the PDF page minus 208 (so book p62 is PDF p270).
- The format is a daily "lesson from today's session", written by a discretionary intraday trader. The markets are mainly E-mini Russell (TF), Euro FX (6E / EUR/USD), GBP/USD, Crude Oil (CL), Hang Seng (HSI), ES and SPI. The timeframes are mostly intraday: 5m higher timeframe, 1m trading timeframe, and 20-tick or 10s for entry. There is one week on **1h/4h/daily forex** (p269–272).
- **Most of the teaching is in chart images, which did not extract.** About half the posts have only a title and one or two lines of text. Where a point depends on the chart, I mark it **[chart]**. Those posts tell us the topic but not the rule.
- Recurring themes: (1) traps and failed expectations ("if price fails to do the obvious, it'll do the opposite"); (2) opening range and prior-day high/low as S/R and as a bias tool; (3) strength/weakness read from swings and candles, not patterns; (4) active trade management (scratch when the premise is in doubt); (5) news risk.

## 3. Detailed notes on technique posts

### 3.1 S/R: how levels are picked, and acceptance vs rejection
- **Picking levels (p122–124).** Take S/R from the higher timeframe. From the current price, look **up** for the next couple of swing highs, plus key congestion-and-breakout areas and gaps; that is resistance. Look **down** for swing lows, congestion-and-breakout areas and gaps; that is support. Nothing else. He doesn't try to grade "accuracy". A level is just a place that showed an imbalance and is emotionally remembered.
  - There are three possible interactions with a level: **test** (holds), **breakout failure**, and **successful breakout**. Your job is to judge which is most likely, using strength/weakness on the approach, then wait for confirmation and fade whoever got it wrong (p124).
- **Default expectation (p156).** A level holds unless strength builds into it. Where reversal patterns form on support *against* strength into the level, the better trade is the break (p190).
- **Mandatory levels (p128).** Prior day's high and low. Prior-day H/L acts as an anchor for how traders judge "high" vs "low". Close to the anchor there is little upside, so buying dries up, profit-taking and with-HTF-trend shorts arrive, and that is how resistance plays out (p300).
- **Other S/R sources.** Breakout points with heavy initiating volume (p11/23). The point where a news spike started (p230). The point of a rapid breakout, which is also the target for a reversal off it (p192). The opening range (p33, p78, p106, p294). Gaps, where he marks the prior close **and** the prior H/L (p158) but cares mainly about the gap to the prior H/L (p303). Trap areas, which are emotional and become later S/R (p226). Failed pre-news breakout levels (p257).
- **Layered S/R (p233).** A break of level 1 that runs straight into level 2 is a candidate for failure and a swing back.
- **Major vs minor S/R (p270–272).** Two methods, demonstrated on a 4H trading timeframe with daily HTF. The method itself is only in the charts. He stresses that "major" is about structure, not quality. **Gap for us:** we need our own definition (see Rule 3).
- **Rejection vs acceptance (p231).** These terms apply only to breaks of S/R or significant swings.
  - Rejection: price can't hold beyond the break. It shows as a tail through the level or a fast two-bar reversal (engulfing, dark cloud).
  - Acceptance: price holds beyond the level. His rough guide is **more than 2 bars** beyond the level means acceptance (he dislikes fixed rules).
  - This gives the trend-change rule on p64: a swing-structure break **plus** acceptance. A gap-fill trade needs acceptance in the direction of the fill first (p150).

### 3.2 Trend qualification (p144, p182) and sideways (p47, p201)
Uptrend features from price action:
1. Rising swing highs and lows.
2. **The largest-range bars are bullish.**
3. Bullish reversal patterns at the ends of pullbacks follow through, while bearish ones fail.
4. "Ledges" (small flat congestions) break upward.

MA features:
- A. Price mostly above the MA.
- B. The MA slopes up.
- C. There is **space** between price and the MA.

Downtrends are the mirror image. A trend model must include a sideways state (p47, p201). In sideways markets, trade only edge tests and BOFs, and avoid trend setups (p201). For MAs he uses EMA(20), or EMA(15)+EMA(20) (p217). The alternative is a channel of **EMA(20) of highs and EMA(20) of lows**: above the channel is bullish, inside is neutral, below is bearish. The channel can also serve as a pullback buy zone or a trailing stop (p217–218).

### 3.3 Strength / weakness and reversal clues
- **Reversal checklist (p100–101).** In an up-move with swings A–E:
  1. Projection shrinks on later extensions.
  2. Bullish momentum falls on later extensions.
  3. Bearish momentum **rises** on each successive pullback.
  4. A standout bearish bar appears: the largest bearish range and highest volume for a long time.
  5. A weak, low-volume bounce fails.
  The formal trigger (a break of the swing low) comes after these.
- **Momentum by time and price (p166).** Compare points per bar across same-direction swings. Example: swing 1 covered 8.2 points in 7 bars; swing 2 covered 7.3 points in 9 bars, so momentum is weaker. This is directly codeable as `|swing price change| / swing bars`.
- **Weakness at resistance (p304–305).** Three pushes into resistance, each with a flatter slope and less projection, then reversal.
- **Close comparison / candle close location (p93–94, p167–168, p306).** Grade each bar by where it closes in its range (low/mid/high close; bull/bear). Example p94: after a break up, the bars closed mid, mid, low, mid, low, then a low-close bear bar. There was no bullishness, so the breakout was rejected and he went short on a CPB. On p168, a pullback whose bars show more counter-trend strength than earlier pullbacks deserves less trust.
- **Failure to follow through (p300–301).** Bearish candles at resistance that don't follow through lower mean sentiment has changed, so look long for the break. A high-volume bull bar that doesn't follow through is bearish absorption (p171–172).
- **Volume (minor role for him).** Rising volume in the trend direction suggests continuation (p180). A volume spike well above recent bars is worth inspecting (p186). Test of demand/supply (p70–71): a wide, high-volume bar on the approach to a range edge, then a retest that breaks its extreme on lower volume, then the turn.
- **Sudden change in behaviour (p262).** The session's largest-range, highest-volume bar marks a sentiment change. A weaker pullback and a stall after it give a textbook pullback entry in the new direction.
- **Extended rallies lead to complex pullbacks (p173).** This repeats ytc.md §5 (far from the MA, expect a CPB).
- **Exhaustive break of a channel return line (p243)** often precedes the end of the channel.

### 3.4 "Fails twice, then the opposite" (p34, p264, p269, p281)
This is the most repeated rule in the collection. Two attempts at the "obvious" move (for example, breaking support) that both stall or fail point to strength the other way.
- p264: two breaks below the session low both stall. The entry is a **buy-stop just above the recent stall**, and the target is the channel high. A later weak retest of the same low gives a second buy-stop entry.
- p269 (1h chart): the second break of support stalls with a doji. Shorts will be stopped above that bar, so enter long on a break above the doji.
- p281: the short after two failed upside attempts was the right trade, but it was scratched when a trap long developed.

### 3.5 BOF vs BPB at a heavily watched level (p87–90)
- The level mattered to breakout traders on every timeframe from 1m to 1h, pattern traders (ascending triangle, cup and handle) were watching it, and there was news.
- His expectation: if news pushed price through, there would be enough orderflow to continue, but he stayed ready for failure.
- A short on an early sign (the break of bar B) is premature. Real failure orderflow only starts once price is **back below the level**, so any early short must be managed aggressively.
- What happened: the pullback was weak, so the BPB entry (break above bar D) was taken, and any premature short was reversed.
- **Rule of thumb:** in BOF trading, the failure isn't confirmed until price closes back inside the level.

### 3.6 Worked BOF trades with numbers (p39–41, p263–264)
- **p39–41.** BOF at support with a pattern trigger. Part 1 was planned at **1:1** and part 2 at **about 3:1**. He wrongly moved the part-1 stop up under a stall and was stopped on a spike. Part 2's target was then cut to the part-1 target because he expected resistance in between. Result: −8 and +13 points instead of +13 and +38. Set-and-forget would have done better here.
  - Rule: **move the stop only to a new invalidation point, or as a conscious scratch.** The stop belongs below the swing-low bar.
- **p263–264.** Strength into support led him to expect a break, so he looked to short a pullback before the break, but the limit order didn't fill. The break stalled about 40 seconds and traded back above the low. There was unexpected buying. A second break stalled too. That is two failures, so he placed a buy-stop above the recent stall, which filled and ran to the channel high. A later weak retest stalled and gave another buy-stop entry.

### 3.7 After a spike / exhaustion (p160–163, p13/24)
- Don't chase a news move. The logical stop is below where the move started, which makes R:R poor (p132). Look for a lower-timeframe entry with acceptable R:R, or let it go.
- After a spike, a **narrow-range inside bar** brackets the stall. On a break up, target where the spike bar started; on a break down, target a retest of the extreme (p161).
- The best counter-trend trade after a spike or exhaustion is often **after a retest of the extreme fails** (a BOF), with the first target at the earlier stall area (p162).

### 3.8 Broadening patterns: partial rise / decline (p235–236)
- A **partial rise** is a rally from the lower edge that stalls short of the upper edge, making a lower high. Bias becomes bearish (expect a break down).
  - Short well above the lower edge, so there is room to scratch if the break doesn't come.
  - Keep the bearish bias until price takes out the swing high of the partial rise.
- A **partial decline** is the mirror image (bullish bias).
- If no in-pattern entry comes, wait for a BPB. Avoid straight breakouts, because they keep failing in this structure. His example did not break out, but the bias was never invalidated.

### 3.9 Volatility contraction setups (p161, p269, p255)
- NR7 (narrowest range of the last 7 bars) and narrow-range inside bars are volatility-expansion setups: bracket them (p161, p269).
- p269 combines a second failed break with a doji/NR7 bar on **1h**.
- For tight congestion, he doesn't take the straight breakout because it usually fails. He waits for the pullback after the break (p255).

### 3.10 Reversal-bar quality (p277–279)
- **Key reversal quality** rises with the number of prior bars the failed side broke. Bars that trap many traders (breaking several bars' lows before closing back up) follow through better. One-tick traps follow through less.
- A key reversal should come at the end of a clear move, a trend or a pullback. In a range it's low quality (p277).
- p278: a key reversal that broke two short-term congestions on rising volume and closed above the prior high is expected to beat a low-volume hook reversal. In fact the hook led to continuation.
- Lesson: pattern influence is short-term, roughly **1 to 6 bars, closer to 1**. What happens after that depends on post-pattern orderflow and on how HTF participants see the move (p279). Same idea on p185: give a pattern about half a dozen bars, and exit earlier if the bars after the trigger show opposite pressure (lower tails under a bearish signal bar, for example).

### 3.11 Entry mechanics
- **Single-bar pullback (p79).** In a strong trend, a one-bar pullback on the trading timeframe is often all you get. Either buy the breakout to new highs, which he doesn't prefer, or use a **lower-timeframe trigger inside that bar** (PAT vol 3 fig 4.65). For us: a one-day pullback in a strong daily trend, with the entry timed on 1h.
- **Fast markets (p45).** Don't use limit orders to buy pullbacks in a ballistic move. Wait for the stall, then use a stop entry in the impulse direction, with smaller size and slippage expected.
- **Volatile periods (p275).** Lower-timeframe micro-pattern breakouts (small double bottoms, ledges, 123s) after a stall in the setup area, while still aiming for a wholesale price.
- **Triggers.** 123 bottom / micro cup-and-handle (p105). Flat-leg 123, called a tap or avalanche (p234). Upthrust (p94). Spring (p29, p240, p288). Micro chart patterns (p260). LWP or pattern trigger (p53). Bracket of a stop below the stall plus a limit above (p267).
- **"Confidence trigger" (p97–99).** For discretionary entries only. Inside a pre-identified setup area, if no entry decision has been made by the time a stochastic-oversold signal fires, enter on that signal. It is not a standalone system.
- **With-trend use of reversal patterns (p113).** A reversal candle at the end of a pullback is a continuation entry.
- **Stand aside in tight, directionless ranges (p176–177).** Mark IF-THEN levels on the HTF that would show sentiment has changed, set alerts, and wait. Or move to another market, or to a smaller timeframe only if the range is wide enough to cover costs.
- **Slow sessions (p103).** The only setups worth taking are failed breaks of significant swing highs/lows or established range edges.

### 3.12 Trade management
- Active, not passive: scratch when the premise is invalid or in doubt (p159, p185). On p159, several pushes into support failed to continue lower, so he scratched and left a pending re-entry order below.
- Multi-part positions let one part pay for a mismanaged part (p39–41). The exit style should match the environment: scalp, hold to target, or run (p164).
- Recovery trades are a planned second entry at the deeper support, used to scratch near breakeven. Only for experienced traders, never on hope (p55).
- Always have a hard stop in the market and attach it to the entry order (p68, p205, p245).

### 3.13 Opening range / gaps / session timing
- The day's high or low is "often" set in the first 5 minutes, citing someone else's stats (p78). The first 5m bar is the session high or low far more than the 1.23% that randomness would give (1 bar of 81, p174).
- Price above the first bar is bullish and below is bearish, as a gross bias (p174, p294–296). The first bar's range is S/R later (p196–197, p290).
- On gap days, set the initial bias from the break of the first bar's range (p290). A gap into prior-day S/R gives a setup plus a tell for the day's direction (p280–281).
- Gaps don't always fill. Need acceptance toward the fill before trading it (p150).
- Breakouts just before a session open or a scheduled event are often traps, so bet on their failure or stand aside (p116, p129, p250, p257–258, p286). "When price fails to do the obvious, expect the opposite" (p250).
- Holiday sessions: default to not trading. If trading, take profits at targets and don't expect runs (p140).

## 4. Mapping to hourly→daily swing trading

Our roles are: **weekly = HTF** (framework), **daily = trading timeframe** (structure, setups, targets), and **1h (or 15m) = entry/LTF**. The blog uses 5m/1m/tick, but says repeatedly that the concepts are fractal (p138, p164). It also has one post on 1h (p269) and one on a 4H trading timeframe with daily HTF (p270).

| Blog idea | Daily/weekly translation | Translates? |
|---|---|---|
| Prior day H/L as mandatory S/R (p128, p300) | **Prior week's H/L** (and prior month's) as S/R for daily trades. For 1h entries, the prior *day's* H/L is the intraday anchor. | Yes. Easy to code. |
| Opening range as bias and S/R (p174, p294) | Two versions. (a) The **first 1h bar of the NSE session** (09:15–10:15) sets bias for a daily-bar entry day: only buy-trigger if 1h price is above the first-hour range. (b) Weekly: the **first daily bar of the week** (or first week of the month) as a gross weekly bias. | (a) yes for 1h-refined entries. (b) speculative; the edge claim is intraday. |
| Gaps: S/R at prior H/L and close (p158, p303); gap into S/R as a tell (p280); fills need acceptance (p150) | NSE stocks gap often. **Gap-open into prior-week H/L or a daily swing level**: does the first 1–2 hours' acceptance or rejection predict the day/week direction? Daily gap edges as S/R. | Yes. Directly applicable to stocks. |
| News spike / pre-news breakout failure (p116, p230, p257) | **Earnings results.** Breakouts in the days before results may fail more often. The bar where a big results move started is S/R. Default: no new entries just before results (p49). | Yes, as a filter and as an event-S/R source. Needs a results calendar. |
| Acceptance >2 bars beyond a level (p231) | Acceptance = 3 daily closes beyond the level. Rejection = a close back inside within 2 bars, or a long tail through the level. | Yes, mechanical. |
| Trend change needs structure break + acceptance (p64) | A daily trend flips only after the confirmed swing break **and** N closes beyond it. | Yes. Also a refinement of our structure-state filter (which showed little edge alone). |
| Trend qualifiers (p144/182) | Daily: largest-range bars in the last N go with the trend; MA slope; "space" (distance from EMA20 in ATR); share of closes above the EMA. | Yes. Candidate *trend-strength score* for momentum-stock selection. |
| Single-bar pullback + LTF trigger (p79) | In a strong daily trend, a **1-day pullback**, then entry on a 1h trigger (break of the prior 1h swing high / 1h micro-123) instead of the daily buy-stop above the prior day's high. | Yes. This is exactly our existing thin edge, with a better (cheaper-risk) entry. |
| Fails twice → opposite (p34, p264, p269) | Daily: two failed closes below a weekly/daily support (or two failed breaks of the 20-day low), then a buy-stop above the second stall bar. | Yes. A double-failure BOF variant. Our single-BOF tests were weak; the two-failure condition is new. |
| Key-reversal quality = bars broken (p277) | Daily: a bar that makes a low under the lows of the last k bars, then closes above the prior bar's high. Larger k should mean better follow-through. | Yes. Mechanical. |
| NR7 / inside bar bracket (p161, p269) | Daily NR7 or inside bar after a pullback in an uptrend: buy-stop above it, stop below it. | Yes. Classic, codeable. |
| Momentum = price/bars per swing (p166); projection shrink (p249, p305) | Daily swings: compare points per bar and projection on the last 2–3 extensions as a trend-exhaustion exit or filter. | Yes (ytc.md §4 already lists these; the blog gives the explicit formula). |
| Candle pattern influence ≈ 1–6 bars (p185, p279) | **Time stop**: if a daily trigger hasn't moved in our favour within about 3–6 bars, exit. With 1h entries, about 6 hourly bars before deciding. | Yes. A cheap rule to test against our trailing exits. |
| Exit style by environment (p164) | Trending (strong qualifiers): trail. Range or weak: fixed target at the prior swing. This matches our finding that T1 at the prior high loses on daily bars and trailing does better. | Partly. Needs an environment classifier. |
| Grind (p252) | Low-volatility persistent drift in a stock. Don't short the first break; short only after a climactic up-bar fails, or after a BPB. | Partly (shorts via F&O). Hard to define "grind". |
| Broadening pattern partial rise (p235) | Weekly/daily megaphone ranges. Rare and hard to detect mechanically. | Weak. |
| EMA20 high/low channel (p217) | Daily channel as a pullback buy zone ("price touched the EMA20(low) band in an uptrend") and as a trailing stop (close below EMA20(low)). | Yes. Simple. |
| Choose moving markets (p241, p282) | Universe selection: rank NSE stocks by ADX or trend score each week and trade only the top movers. | Yes. Likely more important than entry details for us. |
| Stand aside in tight ranges; IF-THEN alert levels (p176) | Skip stocks whose daily range compression has no break yet. Put alerts at the weekly range edges. | Yes (filter). |
| HTF only for structure (p169) vs HTF sentiment as a CPB tiebreaker (p145) | Weekly gives levels (room to the next weekly S/R). Weekly trend is an optional tiebreaker only when the daily CPB breaks the prior swing. | Testable as a conditional filter, not a global one. |

**What doesn't translate**
- Session-specific timing: the first 5 minutes, Chicago PMI leaks, 15-minute open surges (p108), pre-UK-open breakouts.
- Forex holiday rules, range charts, the stochastic "confidence trigger", recovery trades (they need tick-level feel), and multiple fast scratches per session (p199). With costs of about 0.3% round trip, frequent scratching on daily/1h will bleed.
- Tick-level orderflow reads (looking inside the 1-minute bar, p95). The nearest substitute is 15m bars inside a daily bar.

## 5. Testable rules

Conventions:
- Swings are YTC 2-bar pivots, used only once confirmed (ytc.md).
- R = entry − stop.
- Costs are 0.3% round trip.
- **Baseline** = matched random entries: same stock, same month, same direction, same stop distance in ATR, same exit rule. Report the mean R difference and a clustered t-stat by date, as in our existing tests.
- **M** = mechanical. **A** = approximated discretion.

1. **Acceptance-confirmed trend change (p64, p231). M.** Data: daily. A daily uptrend → down only after a close below the confirmed swing low that led to the highest high **and** 3 consecutive closes below it. A break followed by a close back above within 2 bars = "rejected break", and the trend stays up.
   - Test: use it as the state filter for our existing PB long (prior-day-high buy-stop). Compare against the plain structure filter and against random entries.
   - Also test the rejected-break event itself as a long entry (Rule 2).
2. **Rejected swing-low break in an uptrend (p63–64, p93–94, p141). M.** Data: daily (1h optional). Daily uptrend. A bar trades below the last confirmed swing low but closes back above it, or does so within 2 bars. Buy-stop above that bar's high. Stop below its low. Exit: trail with the 2-bar swing low, or a close below EMA20(low).
   - 1h variant: enter on a break of the first 1h swing high after the rejection.
   - Baseline: random long entries in uptrend stocks with the same stop size.
3. **S/R level definition and "room" (p122, p128, p158, p192, p230, p270). M.** Data: weekly, daily. The level set for a stock is:
   - weekly confirmed swing highs/lows;
   - the prior week's H/L;
   - daily gap edges (the prior bar's H/L at the gap);
   - origin bars of rapid moves (a daily bar with range > 2×ATR20; its open is the level);
   - edges of congestion of at least 5 bars followed by a breakout.
   Merge levels within 0.5×ATR into zones. Count "major" = touched ≥ 2 times or weekly origin.
   - Test (a): the hit-rate of price reacting (≥ 1 ATR reversal within 5 bars) at these zones vs random price levels at the same distance.
   - Test (b): the "room to the next zone above, in R" filter on PB longs.
4. **Two-failure BOF (p34, p264, p269, p281). M.** Data: daily, or 1h for the trigger. Two separate attempts (≥ 2 bars apart, within 15 bars) trade below the same support zone (Rule 3, or the 20-day low). Neither attempt has 3 closes below it (no acceptance). Buy-stop above the high of the second attempt's low bar. Stop below the lower of the two lows. Target / exit: the opposite range edge or the prior swing high for part 1, trail the rest.
   - Compare with the single-failure BOF from our earlier tests (which was weak) and with random entries. Shorts are the mirror image via F&O.
5. **Single-bar pullback with 1h trigger (p79, p45, p275). M.** Data: daily, 1h.
   - Setup: daily momentum stock (Rule 10 score in the top quintile) whose last daily bar is a 1-bar pullback (low < prior low, close < prior close) and stays above EMA20.
   - Entry A (current): daily buy-stop above the pullback bar's high.
   - Entry B: on day t+1, buy at the first 1h close above the prior 1h swing high (or above the high of the first hour), with the stop below the 1h pullback low or the daily pullback low, whichever is nearer but still at least 0.5 ATR(daily).
   - Compare R, win% and cost-adjusted expectancy of A vs B vs random. This tests whether 1h refinement turns our thin +0.07–0.10R into more after costs. Note B's tighter stop raises the cost per R.
6. **First-hour bias filter (p174, p294, p290). M.** Data: 1h (needs the first hour, 09:15–10:15). Only allow a long trigger on day t if price is above the first-hour high at the moment of entry, or at least above the first-hour low. Gap days: bias = the direction of the first break of the first-hour range.
   - Test as a filter on Rules 2/4/5 vs unfiltered and vs a random filter of the same pass-rate.
7. **Gap into S/R as a daily tell (p280–281, p150). M.** Data: daily, 1h. The open gaps (> 0.5 ATR) into a resistance zone from Rule 3 (within 0.25 ATR).
   - If the first 2 hours close back below the zone (rejection): short bias for the day/week.
   - If 2+ hourly closes hold above it (acceptance): long bias.
   - Measure the forward 1/3/5-day return conditioned on rejection vs acceptance, vs all gap-ups.
8. **Key-reversal quality (p277–279). M.** Data: daily. Uptrend pullback. A bar's low is under the lows of the prior k bars (k ≥ 2), then it closes above the prior bar's high (key reversal). Enter next-day buy-stop above it. Stop under its low.
   - Bucket by k (1, 2–3, ≥ 4) and by "at the end of a pullback of ≥ 3 bars" vs not. Hypothesis: larger k and pullback context give better follow-through over 1–6 bars.
   - Baseline: random bars with the same k-distribution in non-reversal form.
9. **Pattern time stop (p185, p279). M.** Data: daily (or 1h). For any trigger entry, exit at the close of bar N (N = 3, 5, 6) if the open profit is ≤ 0R, or if the trade has not reached +0.5R.
   - Compare against our trailing exit alone on the same entry set. Watch whether it cuts costs (it adds exits but frees capital).
10. **Trend-strength / "moving market" score (p144, p182, p241, p217). M.** Data: daily. The score combines:
    - fraction of the last 20 bars closing above EMA20;
    - EMA20 slope over 10 bars, in ATR;
    - "space" = (close − EMA20) / ATR;
    - share of the 5 largest-range bars of the last 20 that are bullish;
    - ADX(14).
    Rank the universe weekly. Test whether the top quintile improves PB-long expectancy vs the bottom quintile, and vs our current momentum definition.
11. **Momentum-decay exit / avoid (p166, p249, p100–101, p304). M.** Data: daily. For the last 3 same-direction swings, compute speed = price change / bars and projection = how far the extension ran past the prior swing high, in ATR. If both decline over 2 consecutive extensions, then (a) skip new longs, or (b) tighten the trail to the prior bar's low.
    - Also code the reversal checklist score: + counter-swing speed rising across the last 2 pullbacks + the largest-range bar of the last 20 is bearish.
12. **Volume absorption bar (p171–172, p70–71). M.** Data: daily (volume). A bull bar with volume > 2× the 20-day average and range > 1.5 ATR near resistance (within 0.5 ATR of a Rule-3 zone), and the next 2 bars fail to make a higher close. Hypothesis: bearish. Short on a break of the bar's low, or treat it as a "no long" filter.
    - Mirror case for supply at support. Compare with random high-volume bars not at levels.
13. **NR7 / inside bar continuation (p161, p269). M.** Data: daily. Uptrend (Rule 1 state). After a pullback of 2–5 bars, a bar that is NR7 or an inside bar. Buy-stop above its high, stop below its low. Same trailing exit.
    - Compare with the plain prior-day-high buy-stop. Hypothesis: the tighter stop and better R per trade.
14. **Climactic failure in grind (p252). A.** Data: daily. "Grind" ≈ 15+ bars of persistent up-closes with low ATR relative to its 100-day median and few pullbacks deeper than 1 ATR. Then a climactic bar: range > 2 ATR and a new high. Then it closes below its midpoint within 2 bars. Short via F&O on a break below the climactic bar's low, with the stop above its high.
    - Small sample; mostly exploratory.
15. **Pre-results breakout filter (p116, p257–258, p286, p49). M.** Data: daily plus a results calendar. Breakouts (a close above the 20/55-day high) in the 5 trading days before a results date vs other breakouts: compare 10-day forward R.
    - Hypothesis: higher failure rate. If confirmed, block entries 0–3 days pre-results, and add "failed pre-results breakout" as a BOF candidate.
16. **EMA20 high/low channel zone and trail (p217–218, p152). M.** Data: daily. In an uptrend: the pullback buy zone is the bar's low touching EMA20(high) or lower but closing above EMA20(low). Trail: exit on a close below EMA20(low).
    - Compare with our current pullback definition and trailing stop.
17. **Partial rise/decline bias in ranges (p235–236). A.** Data: weekly, daily. In a daily range (a sideways state from swing structure), a swing up from the lower edge that peaks below 75% of the range height = partial rise, so bearish bias. Short on a break of the next minor swing low, with the stop above the partial-rise swing high.
    - Low sample; exploratory.

## 6. Gaps / discretionary parts

- **About half the posts are chart-only** (for example p36, p83, p114, p126, p188, p238, p270–272, p284, p297, p306). Exact patterns, the second pullback-support clue (p238), the two major-S/R methods (p270) and the strong-breakout signature (p83) can't be recovered from text. If these matter, they need someone to view the PDF images.
- **"Strength into a level"** (p156, p190) decides hold vs break but is never quantified. Our approximations: slope/speed of the approach swing vs prior swings, and closes near the high on the approach.
- **Acceptance "> 2 bars"** is a loose guide; he dislikes fixed rules (p231). We should test 1–4 bars.
- **Active scratching** (p73, p159, p185, p199) is central to his results and is done by feel. Our time-stop and "N opposite-pressure bars" rules only approximate it. On daily bars with 0.3% costs, frequent scratches are expensive.
- **"Bias"** reading (p77, p164, p291) is the core skill, and the blog never makes it objective. The opening-range rule (p294) is the only explicit bias rule, and it is intraday.
- **Environment classification** for exits (p164) is not defined. We'll proxy it with the Rule 10 trend score and a sideways state.
- **Edge claims are anecdotal.** Even the opening-bar statistic is borrowed and unquantified (p78, p174). Every rule above needs our own baseline test. The blog is a source of hypotheses, not evidence.
- **The higher timeframe's role is ambiguous**: structure only (p169) vs sentiment as a tiebreaker (p145). Test both, rather than adopting a global weekly-trend filter (consistent with ytc.md §2).
- **Volume** is minor for him (p186) and is futures volume. NSE delivery or total volume may behave differently.
