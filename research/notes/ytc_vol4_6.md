# YTC Price Action Trader (Lance Beggs): notes on Vol 3 (remainder) to Vol 6

Companion to `ytc.md`, which covers roughly p64–272. These are paraphrased notes, not quotes. Page numbers are **PDF pages** of *YTC Price Action Trader – All 6 Volumes* (the book's own page numbers restart in each volume). Charts don't extract, so wherever a point leans on a figure I say so.

Coverage:
- **p273–476**: Vol 3 remainder. Entry zone (LWP/LRP), entry technique, trade management, the trading process, 10 worked trades, and other markets/timeframes (including stocks).
- **p477–574**: Vol 4, "Your Trading Business". Money management, contingency management, goals and stats, psychology, platform, trading plan, procedures manual, journals.
- **p575–603**: Vol 5, "Trader Development". The journey, deliberate practice, development stages.
- **p604–624**: Vol 6, "Conclusion". Summaries; nothing new apart from restated tables.
- **Additions** from Vol 1/2 intro (p9–63) and a re-check of p215–272 are marked **[addition]**.

YTC's base timeframes are 30 min / 3 min / 1 min on 6B (GBP futures). The roles are: higher TF (HTF) = structure, trading TF (TTF) = trend, setups and targets, lower TF (LTF) = entry timing and early management.

## 1. Section-by-section notes

### 1.0 [addition] Vol 1 intro and Vol 2 ch. 2 (p9–63)
- YTC calls itself a **discretionary swing-trading strategy** across three timeframes. The swings it trades are on the middle (trading) timeframe (p13).
- Profit needs price to move **after** entry, which means others must buy after you. So the aim is to buy where you expect others to be forced to buy later (p45–46).
- The edge comes from traders under **stress**: losers in drawdown, or winners watching profit evaporate. Their behaviour becomes predictable when they exit (p48–49). This is the "trapped trader" idea behind every setup.
- Lagging-indicator signals (MA cross, oscillators) ignore context, in particular whether the signal fires straight into HTF S/R (p51–55, figures). Indicator signals that line up with **a with-trend pullback** do better, because pullbacks draw fresh with-trend orders (p56). This fits our finding that the pullback-in-momentum entry beats random.
- Edge needs all of: setup selection, the ability to take entries, planned position sizing, and trade management skill (p58).
- Subjectivity (p62–63): he argues that fixed objective rules approximate the market and fail at critical moments. If results go wrong mid-session, stop and check whether you are fighting the dominant trend (p63). For us: the book itself warns that a codified version is an approximation.
- **Worked example of the 2-part exit (p50, figure):** take partial profit at the first level where longs are expected to take profit, and send the rest to the next level.

### 1.1 [addition] Re-check of p215–272 (only what `ytc.md` lacks)
- **The expectancy levers are mapped explicitly (p215–217).** Win% comes from setup location, early entry, and active management that keeps profits from being given back. WLSR (average win / average loss) comes from early entry and active management.
- **Context beats pattern (p222–223).** Hunting for setups without HTF awareness gives low-odds trades. His example is a "trap" at a round number that was not real S/R, inside a strong opposing trend. A better entry waits for a clear reversal and then a **weaker retest** of the level.
- **BOF timing (p229).** There is no fixed bar count. Two failed attempts to push after a breakout is a good sign of failure. Beyond 3–5 TTF bars it is rare, and quicker is better.
- **News spikes (p230).** Don't enter until price is moving at normal speed again.
- **Failed BOF = BPB (p232).** A BOF entered before price crosses back over the level must be managed aggressively, ready to scratch and reverse into the BPB.
- **PB failure watch zones (p235–237, figures).** Watch the prior swing high/low area and prior congestion. Fast-move areas (breakouts) become future S/R.
- **CPB (p238–240).** A 3-swing retrace whose 3rd leg breaks the low between legs 1 and 2 traps shorts, which is a strong setup. Extended-duration (grinding) CPBs are hard to trigger. Gain confidence from an LTF trigger at the prior swing area.
- **Over-extension (p241–242).** A trend stretched far from its MA usually needs a CPB before continuing, unless the trend is very fast.
- **Setup choice by environment (p250, repeated p255, p550, p614):**

| Expected environment | Higher probability | Lower probability |
|---|---|---|
| Steady trend | PB, CPB | — |
| Weakening trend | CPB; PB that **breaks the prior swing low** (in an uptrend) and then fails | Plain single-leg PB that breaks no swing; counter-trend TST/BOF at a swing H/L |
| Range with an intra-range trend | — | PB, CPB |
| Approaching HTF/range S/R, no sign of breakout | TST | — |
| Approaching HTF/range S/R with strength | BOF or BPB | — |

- **Counter-trend in strong trends (p250–251).** Always lower probability. Wait for a clear weakening: a deep pullback with more depth than earlier pullbacks and on increased strength, followed by a weaker retest. Even then prefer a BOF over a TST. Use the failure of tempting counter-trend setups as with-trend entries.
- **After S/R holds (p252–253, figures).** With no trend weakness, expect a PB and then a retest. With weakness but no counter-strength, expect a CPB and then a retest. With counter-strength, treat the retest as a PB in the new direction.
- **Don't trade between setup areas (p256).** He expects 1–2 setups an hour on 3 min. If that is too slow, use a lower timeframe rather than force trades.
- **Stop placement detail (p259–263).**
  - Stops are **tentative** and move as the pullback extends ("watch the bears show you all they've got").
  - Prefer the stop below **all** recent swing lows if R:R still works; otherwise use the nearer one (p262).
  - How far beyond the level depends on your confidence that the level holds. His buffer is two ticks (p263).
  - The stop is a **worst-case** stop; active management exits earlier (p263).
- **Targets (p264–269).**
  - He calls fixed 2R/3R targets "rubbish" (p264). Targets come from opposing orderflow.
  - With no HTF level overhead (a month-long uptrend, or ATH), exit at the first stall above T1 or trail to a far level (p266).
  - In ranges, use T1 = T2 (p267).
- **Entry uncertainty (p270–272, figures).** Whether a stop entry or a limit entry is better can't be known until afterwards. Smooth markets lean toward stop entries, choppy overlapping ones toward limit entries, but that is not reliable. He adds that management and exit matter more than entry (p272).

### 1.2 Wholesale entry: LWP, LRP and the entry zone (p273–281)
- **Wholesale vs retail (p273–274, figures).** Swings have limited room. Enter early, at the "wholesale" part of the swing, rather than at breakout-of-swing-low style "retail" prices that raise risk and cut potential.
- **Last Wholesale Price (LWP) (p275–277).** This is the breakout of the TTF reversal pattern or congestion in the setup area: the point where trapped traders' stops start to fire. Everything at or before it is wholesale; anything after is retail.
  - **Never chase beyond the LWP.** If you miss it, wait for a second-chance retest or the next setup (p275, p277).
  - Examples on p275–276 are figure-based. BPB: break below a low-close range candle after two bull candles at the breakout point. Second-chance BOF: break above the last low-close bear candle. TST at resistance: a low-close bear candle that fully reverses a big bull candle. PB: above a green lower-tail reversal candle. CPB: above a two-candle reversal pattern. TST of range support: break of a pause.
- **R:R used correctly (p278–279).** Find the stop (from price action) and the target (from structure) first. Then derive the **Last R:R Price (LRP)**, the furthest entry that still meets the R:R you want. The common mistake is to set a fixed multiple of risk without checking that the target is reachable (p278).
- **His R:R (p280).** **1:1 to T1 for part one**, so the LRP is the **midpoint between stop and T1**. That is acceptable because (a) active scratching cuts the realised loss and (b) part two runs further.
- **Entry zone (p280, figure).** From the stop up to whichever of LWP or LRP is closer.
- Don't compute LWP/LRP precisely intraday; eyeball them (p281).
- **Entry preconditions checklist (p281).**
  1. Price is in a setup area, fading weakness at S/R or a swing H/L, or on a pullback.
  2. The stop is identified (ideally beyond S/R or a swing H/L).
  3. Targets are identified (next TTF swing or S/R).
  4. The entry zone is known.

### 1.3 Entry technique (p282–298)
- **Simplified entry (p282).** Place a **stop-entry order at the LWP** as soon as it is identified. Move it if a better LWP forms; cancel it if the setup is invalidated. **Only if the R:R is ≥1:1.** It gives a worse price than an actively worked entry, but it is "still effective", because the LWP is where orderflow should start helping. It suits developing traders. Practise better entries on replay later.
- **Active entry (p283–290).** Two triggers on the LTF:
  - **Stall (p284–286).** Price clearly fails to continue beyond setup S/R, usually at least 3 TTF candles. This allows a **limit order near the S/R** at a deeply discounted price. The cost: it can fill and then lose where a stop at the LWP would never have triggered (p285). Example p285–286: the LWP was too close to T1 (14 pips risk for 2 pips reward), so only a limit at or before the LRP was acceptable.
  - **Pattern triggers (p286–290; restated p616–619).** Any LTF pattern that confirms short-term sentiment and defines a stop:
    - candlestick reversals;
    - bar reversals;
    - spike-and-ledge;
    - double top/bottom (enter on confirmation of the 2nd low, **no later than** the swing-high break);
    - spring/upthrust (a spike beyond S/R reversed within 1–2 candles; trigger on a break of the spring candle's high);
    - 3-swing retrace;
    - 123 top/bottom;
    - test of breakout point;
    - expansion bar (a larger-range momentum bar after a retracement to the MA).
- **Order types (p291).** He prefers limit orders and adjusts them; you may prefer stop entries. **Never market orders for entry** (slippage).
- Example p292: a traditional double-bottom entry (the swing-high break) would have been beyond the LWP, so it was invalid even though it would have worked.
- Example p293: sometimes the LTF trigger coincides with the LWP and adds only confidence.
- **Bracketing (p294–295).** If the LWP is inside the entry zone, place both a **stop entry at the LWP** (so you can't miss the move) and a **working limit order** for a better price. When one fills, cancel the other. If the LWP is outside the zone, don't place a stop entry, and don't place one at the LRP either (it is not a technical level). Just work the limit and accept missing the trade.
- **Missed entries (p295).** Never chase. Log the miss for post-session review and replay.
- **Scaling (p295).** He enters all-in and scales out. As an alternative to test, scale in: part one on a worked limit, part two on a stop at the LWP.
- **Summary and warning (p298).** The TTF defines where to trade, the LTF when. Early limit entries lack the trapped-trader fuel until the LWP trades, so they can be stopped before the LWP is reached. Use scratching for early entries that don't follow through past the LWP.

### 1.4 Trade management (p299–318)
- **Active vs passive (p299–302), important for us.** He says plainly that a **mechanical trader with a backtested edge should manage exactly as tested** (hold to stop/target, or fixed stop-movement rules) and should not use discretion (p299–300). He manages actively because:
  - he trades small daily samples and dislikes weekly or monthly drawdowns;
  - he sees the market's probabilities as shifting;
  - his edge exists only while the trade premise is valid (p300–301).
  - He does not claim active management is optimised for results; it suits his beliefs and psychology (p301).
  - Passive management needs "thorough testing" (p302).
- **Structure (p302).** Two-part position: all-in entry, scale-out exit. Part 1 targets T1. Part 2 targets T2 or trails, depending on the environment.
- **Exit before target only when one of three things happens (p302–303).**
  1. Bar-by-bar evidence of a sentiment change invalidates the premise. Exit and move on.
  2. **The market fails to move your way after a reasonable time.** The premise hasn't been invalidated, but its odds have dropped. Exit and reassess.
  3. You are unsure of the market state. Exit and reassess.
  4. Otherwise **hold**.
- **Pre-plan scenarios (p303).** Before each bar, decide what you will do if price goes up, goes down or stalls.
- **Timeframe for management (p303–304).** Start on the LTF near the entry zone and move to the TTF as soon as you can. Managing on the LTF whipsaws you. Hint: once part 1 hits T1, manage part 2 on the TTF to give it room.
- **Drawdown right after entry is normal (p304).** You can't pick the best entry. Pre-define what a valid retest looks like versus one that will take out the stop.
- **Over-management (p304–305).** His biggest problem is over-managing around the entry. In hindsight he usually should have been more patient. He uses a short mantra: patience, trust, where's the strength/weakness, hold while the premise is valid.
- **Scratching (p305–308).**
  - Exiting also restores objectivity: you can reassess without an open position (p305).
  - Two methods (p306–307, figures): (1) close immediately when urgently threatened; (2) work the exit by tightening the stop and/or pulling the target to nearby levels taken from the LTF.
  - **Multiple parts (p307).** If the premise is invalid, scratch everything. If you are only doubtful, manage separately: tighten part 1's target to just before the next swing high and trail it tightly, while **part 2 keeps its original stop and target** in case the doubt is wrong.
  - Example p307–308: a BOF ahead of a news release, worked to part 1 at breakeven and part 2 at +5 pips.
- **Re-entry (p309–310).**
  - Allowed after a scratch, after part 1 has taken profit (a "new part one"), or after a stop-out.
  - **Only on a valid trigger**: it must be a trade you would take as a first entry.
  - Unlimited entries per setup area, but **at most two full-size losses** (or the equivalent in money) per setup area. After that your read is out of sync, so let it go (p309).
  - Example p310: a precautionary scratch for −2 pips, then a re-entry that worked.
- **Stop to breakeven and beyond (p311–313).**
  - Move to BE **when you would no longer want to be in the trade if price came back to entry**. Too early caps the upside through normal noise (p311).
  - Beyond BE: move to levels you would not want price to revisit, i.e. beyond **new swing lows** or beyond **large with-trend candles that make new highs** (p311).
  - Parts can differ: part 1 more aggressive, part 2 given more room (p311).
  - Example p312 (figure): part 1 goes to BE after the first surge-and-retrace. Then it trails below the last push-down before new highs. Near T1 it tightens **purely for R:R**: with 4 pips of reward left against 16 pips of risk, the stop came up (p312–313).
  - Rule of thumb (p313): **if price stalls just short of the target, take the profit**. Treat targets as zones, not exact prices.
- **Erroneous entry (p313).** Don't exit by reflex. If there is some short-term potential, manage it very aggressively; if not, exit fast.
- **Part two (p314–318).**
  - Same principles with more room. Trail beyond new TTF swing lows or large with-trend TTF candles (p314).
  - Example p315: part 1 to BE early, because a second failure at the same zone would not be tolerated on a re-entry. At T1, part 2 goes to BE, then trails above each TTF low-close bear candle.
  - **Adapt to the environment (p316).** In a trend, let part 2 run. In a range, running is low-odds, so stick to T2 or even set T1 = T2. If the entry appears to be near a session high/low (a turning point) in a trending market, give it more room, even back to BE.
  - **Extending the target (p316–318).** If strength builds, remove T2 and trail behind swing lows and large bull candles. Example p317–318: part 1 +21, part 2 +83 pips.

### 1.5 The trading process checklist (p319–324; repeated in the procedures manual p555–557)
Four steps (the figure is on p319):
1. **Preparation.** Confirm price behaves as anticipated; find stop, T1, T2, entry zone (LWP/LRP) and the order plan; do a final validity check for this environment.
2. **Entry.**
   - Watch bar by bar; place the bracket or worked order.
   - **Confirm the pending-order details**: price, direction, size, attached stop and target.
   - On fill, cancel redundant orders and confirm a full or partial fill.
   - Do a post-fill gross-error check: how does the entry feel, and is it still valid.
3. **Management and exit.**
   - Per part: BE and trailing rules while the premise holds.
   - If threatened, cancel immediately or work the exit.
   - For part 2, consider extending T2 or switching to a trail on strength.
   - Confirm fills; **confirm flat** at the end.
4. **Post-trade.**
   - Reset the order screens.
   - After a loss, consider a recovery process.
   - If a re-entry is setting up, delay the paperwork.
   - Update the log with observations on market structure, your process, and your physical and psychological state.

Conclusion (p323–324): conceptually it is just buying pullbacks in uptrends, selling pullbacks in downtrends, and fading tests of S/R. The skill is in the nuances, built through deliberate practice.

Observations:
- Across these examples the **runner (part 2) made about 2.4× the pips of part 1**: 259 vs 106. They are selected examples, but the book claims they are realistic.
- Initial risk was 6–17 pips. T1 was usually ≥1R and often 2–4R.
- He states the majority of real trades are **scratches, small wins or small losses** (p450).
- Recurring management pattern:
  - Part 1 goes to BE once price makes new highs or is near T1.
  - Part 2 goes to BE when T1 is hit, then trails behind each with-trend TTF candle or swing (p336, p350, p375, p433, p448).
  - Close to target, tighten (p387, p448).
  - Slowing momentum is handled by moving the stop rather than the target, which keeps the upside open (p448).
- **Don't reverse often (p435).** Each trade must stand on its own merits; a failure one way is not a signal for the other way.
- Example 10 (p446): after one attempt short and one long, step back and wait for clearer direction.

### 1.7 Other markets and timeframes, including stocks (p452–476)
- **Requirements (p453–454).** Liquidity (avoid charts with gaps from missing trades) and **average swing size well above costs**. Example: a 15 min EUR/USD swing of about 30 pips against a 2 pip spread works; a 1 min swing of about 6 pips against the same spread does not. **The higher your costs, the higher the timeframe you need.**
- Base intraday setup needs half to a full session at the screen and averages about **1 trade per hour** (p453).
- **Timeframe triplets shown** (p456–460, p464–467, p471–473). He stresses these are **demonstrations, not recommendations**, to be chosen by your own testing (p476).

| Market | HTF / TTF / LTF | Page |
|---|---|---|
| GBP/USD (his own until 2009) | 60–30m / 5m / 1m | p456 |
| GBP/USD | 60m / 15m / 5m | p457 |
| EUR/USD | Daily / 4h / 1h (PB×3 and a TST shown; LTF entries on 1h, figures p459) | p458–459 |
| EUR/JPY ("fits around a job") | Weekly / Daily / 4h | p460 |
| YM, ES, TF emini | 30m / 3–5m / 1m; scalping 5m / 1m / 20-tick | p464–467 |

| SPY, SBUX, OSX intraday | 1h / 5m / 1m | p473 |

- **Spread (p461).** A fixed spread is a large handicap on low timeframes and matters much less on higher ones. Only the high volatility/spread pairs work at 5/1 min.
- **Number of instruments (p462).** On 30/3/1 he focuses on one instrument, two at most. Higher-timeframe traders can monitor more. You can scan a basket and choose 1–2 per session by a criterion that suits the environment you prefer, trending or ranging.
- **Session opens (p462–463, p469).** Treat the open like a just-broken S/R level. Read strength or weakness in the first minutes before committing, and don't hurry until your bias is clear.
- **News (p463, p470).**
  - Don't **start** a trade just before a market-moving release unless it should be finished or well in profit by then.
  - Close or tighten open trades before the release.
  - Risk management is the top priority.
- **Gap opens (p469–470, stocks p475).**
  - No fixed rules; judge each open.
  - Build structure first from the session-only chart (which shows the daily gaps), plus significant overnight levels.
  - Hold off trading until the bias is established, watching the opening candles on the LTF.
- **Stock selection (p475).** He hasn't traded stocks for years and gives no definitive method. His suggestion:
  1. Scan for **trending stocks that meet a minimum volume**.
  2. Visually narrow to the **20–30 with the smoothest trends**. That is the daily watchlist.
  3. **Refresh it each weekend**, dropping names that no longer qualify and swapping the worst for better ones.
  4. Or outsource the list to a "most active" list.
  5. Opportunity comes from movement.
- **Multiple positions (p476).** On higher timeframes you will hold several at once, so set a cap on total exposure (detailed p486–487).
- **Stock news (p476).** You must research stock, sector and market events (earnings and so on) and manage that risk.

### 1.8 Vol 4 ch. 7: money management (p477–488)
- **Survival first (p479).** You fail only by quitting or being forced out. Financial and personal survival both matter. Big losses make you risk-averse and damage future execution (p479).
- **Five risks to control (p479–480):**
  1. single-trade loss;
  2. session drawdown;
  3. business drawdown, i.e. a run of losses;
  4. increased-size risk, i.e. being unable to handle larger size psychologically;
  5. insufficient income forcing withdrawals.
- **Example plan (p481), with explanations on p482–487:**
  - **Per trade: max risk 1% of equity.** With two parts, **0.5% per part** (p482). He calls the popular 2% too much while learning, since five straight losses would cost 10% (p482). Never let a trade run past its stop (p482).
  - **Gap/slippage warning (p482).** A stop becomes a market order and can slip badly in a panic. The 1% cap is the buffer.
  - **Session timeout at 2% drawdown from the session's equity high; compulsory session stop at 3%** (p481, p483).
    - Two full losses suggest you are out of sync. Take a break and review.
    - Continue only if the trades followed the plan or the errors are fixed (p483).
    - With active scratching, reaching 2% may take 2–5 or more trades (p483).
    - **Hard ceiling: never more than 3% timeout and 5% stop** (p483).
  - **These are trailing limits (p483–484).** Both trail the intra-session equity high, so a good start isn't given back.
  - A **session profit target** is optional and he doesn't use one (p484).
  - **Fixed money amounts recomputed each weekend** from the previous week's closing equity (p481, p484). Worked example (p484): $20k equity gives $200 risk per trade ($100 per part), a $400 trailing timeout and a $600 trailing stop.
  - **Business stop at 20% drawdown, absolute max 30%** (p481, p484). Then:
    - review the plan and the performance;
    - go back to sim until profitable again;
    - small accounts should top the account back up before going live again (p485).
  - **Graduated size increases (p481, p485).** Each increase requires:
    - (a) a profitable month at the current size;
    - (b) equity that allows it within the 1% rule;
    - (c) a profitable sim week at the new size.
    - After one session stop, consider stepping back.
    - **After two session stops with no new equity high in between, you must go back to the previous size.**
  - **Income (p481, p485–486).** Don't rely on trading income until consistently profitable; withdrawals put the business at risk. Trade around a job with limited hours, e.g. **daily charts** or one session open (p486). Full-time only once trading regularly makes about twice the working income (p481).
- **Higher timeframe / multiple markets (p486–487), the key part for us.**
  - **Total open risk across positions ≤ 3%.** That can mean 6 positions at 0.5% each.
  - Positions whose **stop is at breakeven or better no longer count**, so you can add new ones as old ones reach BE (p486).
  - **Correlation:** two correlated positions at 1% each are really one 2% bet. Avoid highly correlated positions unless you actively monitor correlation (p487).
  - **Redefine "session"** for multi-day trades, e.g. **weekly timeout and stop limits** (p487).
  - An intermediate weekly or monthly limit between the 3% session stop and the 20% business stop is optional. He doesn't use one (p487).
- **If capital can't support 1% risk**, stay in sim, or pick a smaller contract or market. Don't raise the risk (p487).
- **Avoid optimal-f / Kelly; keep it simple (p487–488).** The focus is capital preservation while learning.

### 1.9 Ch. 8: contingency management (p489–492; repeated in the procedures manual p561–563)
Method: list the threats and errors, then write a treatment for each that avoids them or limits the damage (p490). It is based on aviation threat-and-error management. His list:
- **Illness, stress or negativity (p490–491).** Close trades and cancel orders, or, if trades are in profit, make sure the stops are ≥ breakeven and the targets are set, then walk away. Cancel working orders, log it, and consider a break or ending the session.
- **External distraction (p491).** Same as above, then deal with the distraction and log it.
- **Wrong order or wrong fill (p491).** Cancel if unfilled. If filled: exit if the market can move fast against it; otherwise set an aggressive stop and work an exit. Log it. Contact the broker if it was the broker's error.
- **Connectivity loss in a trade (p492).** Flatten through whatever route is fastest: backup PC, web platform, or phone/chat with the broker. **Every order must have an attached stop and target.**
- **Drawdown limit hit (p492).** **Exit all trades immediately**, take a break, run the post-session routine.
- **Rule violation (p492).** No new trades. Close what should be closed. Manage open trades to exit. Log it. Take a break.

### 1.10 Ch. 9: goals, targets and stats (p493–499)
- **Expected win%/WLSR (p494).** For discretionary traders these depend on the strategy **and** on personal execution. For most new traders execution destroys the edge. Only your own figures matter. **Don't trade live until your performance shows positive expectancy.** Mechanical traders get their targets from backtests (p494).
- **If you must have a target (p495): win% about 65% and WLSR about 1.5, excluding breakeven trades.** The two trade off against each other; any positive-expectancy pair will do.
- **Stats to keep (p495–496):**
  - win% and WLSR overall;
  - by **setup** (TST/BOF/BPB/PB/CPB), by **instrument**, by **session**, by **weekday**, plus any category you find useful.
- **Review weekly and monthly, with at least 20 trades per sample.** If a week has fewer than 20, roll it into the next (p496).
- Stats confirm consistency when they match your history, and warn you when they drift (p496).
- **Improving win%:** check setup quality (with strength, against weakness, someone trapped) and **limit yourself to your best-performing setups**. His "best" setups: a **PB or CPB that broke a prior swing high/low and then reversed, or any BPB** (p496).
- **Improving WLSR:** look at entry lateness and whether parts 1 and 2 are allowed to reach their targets (p496). His spreadsheet calls WLSR "profit factor" (p496). Note: that is not the usual definition of profit factor.
- **Per-session targets for consistently profitable traders (p496–497):** 70% of days positive, 90% of weeks, 100% of months, with individual trades irrelevant. This needs at least 5 trades a day and 20 a week (p497).
- **Probabilistic framing (p497–499, figures).** Simulated equity curves with 70% and 60% winning days, average win equal to average loss, both rise.

### 1.11 Ch. 10: psychology, practical (p500–522)
- **Prerequisites (p501–505):**
  - realistic expectations: likely years, so structure life so that success has no deadline;
  - a documented "reason why" (pain/pleasure, or four motivating emotions);
  - you must **enjoy the process**.
- **The chain (p506).** A negative mindset leads to poor decisions, then inconsistent application of the plan, then failure. So manage decisions through process.
- **Focus on process (p506–508):**
  - documented procedures;
  - **daily process goals, not outcome goals**. Examples: every entry has an attached stop and target; only with-trend trades today (p507).
  - The goal comes from the post-session review, is written in the log and is read in the next pre-session (p508).
  - **A timer beeps at each TTF candle close** to pull attention back (p508).
  - A monthly review of notes for longer-term focus patterns (p508).
- **Peak-performance tools (p509–516):**
  - breathing/relaxation before, during and after the session;
  - visualising the process goal, with both a winning and a losing outcome counted as a success if the process was followed (p510–511);
  - a motivation journal: reasons, current reminders, goals, proof of good trades, motivational material (p511–512);
  - **Action Affirmation statements** for each phase: Focus / Analysis / Entry / Management / Regroup (p513–515; repeated p564–565);
  - a **recovery procedure** after a significant loss or strong emotion: relax, review the feelings and the lesson, release; 10–15 min (p515–516).
- **Fatigue (p517–522).**
  - Sleep diary (p519).
  - **Acute fatigue go/no-go: <5 h sleep in the last 24 h, or <12 h in the last 48 h, or awake longer than the sleep in the last 48 h. If any applies, don't trade** (p520).
  - Symptoms include errors in stops, size or direction (p520).
  - Others notice fatigue before you do (p521).

### 1.16 Vol 5 ch. 15–16: the journey and the learning process (p575–589)
- **Most readers will fail (p577).** Industry failure is quoted at 80–95%+. Every setup varies endlessly, which breeds doubt. Figure 15.1 (p577–578) shows six variants of price at S/R: TST stopped then BOF, extended hold below support, failed BOF then BPB, falling short of the level, smashing through, grinding sideways.
- **Results depend on three skills (p578–579):** perceiving strength/weakness in HTF context; timing entries and exits within setup areas; and trusting yourself enough to act without hesitating.
- **You have to become a trader, not acquire a system (p579–580).** Knowing when to take or skip a setup, limit vs breakout entry, when to scratch.
- **Deliberate practice (p582–583).** Cycle: pick a target, reach, evaluate the gap, repeat. One quoted training exercise: study **several hundred daily-chart examples of S/R holding and failing**, then look at the intraday price action around them (p583).
- **Trade–Record–Review–Improve cycle (p583–584).** The review asks four questions:
  1. What did you expect?
  2. What happened?
  3. Why the difference?
  4. What is the lesson?
  - "Improve" means process goals or amendments to the plan or procedures.
- **Tools (p584–589):**
  - defined procedures;
  - logs and journals;
  - a **documented review process** whose core is **your performance vs hindsight-perfect performance** (p585);
  - **market replay**: re-trade the session as it happened, first repeating your actual decisions to feel how wrong they were, then trading the hindsight-perfect version (p585–588; worked through Example 7, p586–587);
  - alternatives to replay: screen recording, or stepping bar by bar manually (p588);
  - peer review in a group of 4–6, swapping journals (p588–589).

### 1.17 Ch. 17: taking action, the development stages (p590–603)
Four stages (the figure is on p591). Each has explicit targets to clear before advancing:
- **Stage 1, foundation (p592–594).**
  - Read the books; choose market and timeframe; write the plan, manual and journals; set up the platform.
  - **Study 100 historical sessions** of printed charts on all three timeframes with hindsight: HTF S/R, trend changes, how price moves, setup areas, holding vs failing, simple vs complex pullbacks. **No trades at this stage** (p592–593).
  - **Then 2+ weeks (10 sessions) on a simulator, following price without trading** (p593).
  - **Then revisit the 100 sessions for trade opportunity**: ideal entry, scratch, BE, exit (p593).
  - **Then replay the sim sessions and trade every setup** (p594).
  - At least 50 sessions must come from the market you will actually trade (p594).
- **Stage 2, simulator (p595).** One week as a benchmark, then improve **until 4 consecutive profitable weeks**.
- **Stage 3, live at minimum size (p596).**
  - **Stop trading if 10% of the trading funds is lost**, then review and go back to sim. You must be able to afford that 10%.
  - No size increase until 4 consecutive profitable live weeks.
- **Stage 4, increasing size (p597).**
  - Prove the new size in sim for a week first.
  - If that fails, retry once. If it fails again, stay at the old size; you advance only if the combined two weeks are profitable.
  - Then you need 4 profitable live weeks at each size level.
  - Increases must be gradual.
- **A "week" means at least 20 trades; otherwise merge weeks** (p597).
- **Alternate strategies (p598).** If you build your own, first watch price at S/R, swings and pullbacks, then go through the same four stages.
- **Challenges (p599–603):**
  - **Reduce size first**, to almost meaningless levels, and return to sim if needed (p599).
  - **Simplify.** Use the records to find your strongest setups and environments, **trade only those, and drop the weakest**. Examples: with-trend only, or BOF only (p599–600).
  - **Two timeframes with simple entries.** Drop the LTF and enter at the LWP on the TTF breakout if the R:R is OK (p600).
  - **Right focus.** Too many low-probability trades usually means you are hunting on the LTF; keep the TTF central (p600–601).
  - **"One good trade".** Write a checklist of what a "good trade" is and take only those (p601).
  - **Order-placement AA statements**: confirm setup, stop, target and R:R, then place the order far away and work it closer (p601–602).
  - **Team of roles.** The Risk Manager **vetoes any trade without acceptable R:R and enforces drawdown limits**. The Order Clerk executes without second-guessing. A Compliance role reviews (p602–603).
- **The target is never reached; the cycle continues (p603).**

## 2. Mapping to hourly→daily swing trading and to our app

### 2.1 Timeframes
- The book's own stock example is **Weekly / Daily / 1h** (p471–472), which matches our plan. Weekly gives the S/R framework and T2; daily gives trend, setup, stop and T1; 1h gives entry triggers and early management.
- An alternative from p458 is Daily / 4h / 1h, for a faster variant with more setups.
- He says timeframes must be chosen by your own testing (p476). Lower timeframes also need lower costs (p454).
- **Cost check for NSE.** Daily swings on liquid stocks are large relative to brokerage and STT, so they pass p454. 15m swings may not. Our first test should measure average daily-swing size vs round-trip cost per stock, and again for 1h swings.
- **Management timeframe (p303–304, p314).** Manage on the LTF (1h) near entry. Switch to the TTF (daily) as soon as possible, and certainly once part 1 has hit T1. For us that means 1h-based scratch, BE and tight-trail rules only on the entry day or the first 1–2 sessions, then daily-bar rules.

### 2.2 What carries over to a systematic swing strategy
- **Key point (p299–300):** YTC itself says a mechanical trader must manage exactly as tested. Our app is systematic, so every management idea below has to become a fixed rule, be backtested, and then be applied without discretion.
- **The entry zone (LWP/LRP) is the most codeable new idea.** It may explain why our "T1 at prior swing high" exit lost on daily bars.
  - Our buy-stop above the prior bar's high is essentially a **simplified LWP entry** (p282).
  - But YTC only takes it when entry ≤ the LRP, i.e. **reward to T1 is ≥ risk to stop** (p280, p282).
  - Without that gate, many of our trades probably entered with T1 well under 1R away. That makes a fixed T1 a loser by construction.
  - Test: apply the 1:1-to-T1 gate, or skip T1 entirely when the gate fails.
- **No chasing (p275, p295).** On daily bars, if the stock **gaps open above the buy-stop by more than X, or above the LRP**, skip the trade rather than fill at the open. This is directly codeable, and important for NSE gaps.
- **Two-part position.** Part 1 at T1 (only when T1 ≥ 1R); part 2 trails. Our finding that trailing beats fixed exits matches YTC's emphasis on the runner: about 2.4× the pips in the examples (p325–449), and "remove T2 and trail when strong" (p316–317). The no-HTF-level case (52-week highs or ATH) means trail with no target (p265–266). Momentum stocks are often in exactly that state.
- **Trailing method (p311, p314, p336, p350, p433, p448).** Trail beyond each new confirmed swing low on the TTF, or beyond each large with-trend TTF bar that makes a new high. These are codeable: "after a daily bar closes in the top third of its range at a new high, move the stop to just below that bar's low", or "below the latest confirmed 2-bar swing low".
- **Breakeven rule (p311, p315, p336, p432).** Part 2 goes to BE when T1 fills. Part 1 goes to BE when price makes a new high after entry, or when it is near T1. Warning: too early a BE gets stopped by noise (p311, p387).
- **Time-based scratch (p302–303, rule 2).** If the trade hasn't moved in your favour after a reasonable time, exit. Codeable as "exit at close if after N daily bars MFE < k·R" or "price hasn't exceeded the entry bar's high".
- **Follow-through scratch (p408, p422).** An early entry that fails to reach or hold beyond the LWP within a bar or two gets scratched. On daily data: if the day after entry closes back below the entry trigger, or below the signal bar's midpoint, exit.
- **R:R tightening near target (p312–313, p387).** When the remaining reward is a small fraction of the distance to the stop, tighten the stop or just take the profit. Codeable.
- **Environment-adaptive T2 (p316).** In a trend, let it run; in a range, T2 = T1. This needs a regime classifier (our structure state, or the efficiency ratio).
- **Setup filters (p250, p496).** Highest-quality pullbacks are the ones where **someone is trapped**: a PB/CPB that **broke the prior swing low and then reclaimed it**, or a BPB. Our "depth/duration alone has no edge" finding doesn't cover this trap condition. Test "pullback undercut the prior swing low or the low of the first leg, then a daily close back above it" separately.
- **Over-extension (p241–242).** Far from the MA, wait for a CPB instead of the first PB. Codeable as distance from the 20/50 EMA in ATR units.
- **Counter-trend shorts (p250–251).** With F&O shorts allowed, YTC's view is: no counter-trend shorts in strong uptrends. Only after a deep, strong pullback followed by a weaker retest of the high, preferably as a BOF. **Shorts should mainly be with-trend PB/CPB in established downtrends.**
- **Events (p463, p470, p476).** Don't open a swing trade just before **earnings**, RBI policy or the Budget unless it would be done or well in profit by then. Close or tighten held positions before them. Gap opens: no 1h entries in the first hour of a gap day until the bias is clear (p469, p475).
- **Universe (p475).** Each weekend, scan trend plus minimum volume, keep the 20–30 smoothest trends, and refresh weekly. "Smoothness" can be quantified, e.g. efficiency ratio, R² of the log-price regression, or fewer overlapping bars.

### 2.3 Risk controls (portfolio level)
- Per trade: risk ≤ 1% of equity (≤ 0.5% per part) (p481–482).
- **Total open risk ≤ 3%.** Positions with stops at BE or better count as zero, so new positions are allowed as old ones reach BE (p486). For a swing book of about 6 positions, that means 0.5% each.
- **Correlation (p487).** Count same-sector or high-correlation positions as one bet, with sector caps or correlation-adjusted risk. Index-heavy NSE large caps are highly correlated, so this matters.
- **Redefine "session" as a week for multi-day trading (p487).** A weekly timeout at 2% drawdown from the week's equity high (review before new entries) and a weekly stop at 3% (no new entries until next week). Both trail the equity high (p483–484). Hard ceilings are 3% and 5% (p483).
- **Business stop at 20% drawdown, max 30%.** Then go back to paper trading (p481, p484).
- **Live minimum-size stage: stop at a 10% loss of funds** (p596).
- Risk amounts recomputed each weekend from the last close equity (p481, p484, p566).
- **Gap risk (p482).** Stops can slip in a panic; for stocks that includes overnight gaps. Size on stop distance, but also monitor actual-vs-planned loss slippage.
- Size increases are graduated and gated on profitable months (p481, p485, p597).
- **No Kelly or optimal-f** (p487).

### 2.4 Paper trading and the review loop in the app
- The development stages (p592–597) map directly onto our deployment:
  1. backtest and hindsight study, i.e. many historical setups reviewed;
  2. **paper trading until 4 consecutive profitable "weeks" of ≥20 trades each** (for a swing strategy that may be blocks of 20 trades rather than calendar weeks; p597);
  3. live at minimum size with a 10% kill switch;
  4. size up only after a paper-proven increase plus 4 profitable blocks.
- **Replay** (p585–588) means the app should be able to step through bars and re-simulate a trade, including alternative management, so the actual result can be compared with the hindsight-ideal one.
- **Review vs hindsight-perfect (p558–560).** For every trade, store the ideal entry/stop/exit computed afterwards: e.g. MFE/MAE, best trail exit, optimal BE point. Also store **missed signals** (signals generated but not taken, e.g. gap-skipped or risk-cap-skipped) with their outcomes.
- Stats by subset (p495–496): setup type, stock, sector, long/short, weekday, regime. Minimum 20 trades per reporting sample.

## 3. Testable rules

Notation: TTF = daily, HTF = weekly, LTF = 1h (or 15m). R = entry−stop distance.
- "M" = mechanical as written in the book.
- "A" = approximation of discretion.
- Baseline for every rule: our current momentum-pullback buy-stop entry with its existing exit, plus the matched random-entry baseline (same stock, same day distribution, same stop/exit logic). Evaluate in R after costs, using clustered t-stats as before.

1. **LRP gate: ≥1:1 reward to T1 (p279–280, p282).** M.
   - Rule: stop = pullback low − buffer; T1 = prior swing high (the confirmed 2-bar pivot). Take the buy-stop only if (T1 − entry) ≥ (entry − stop). Otherwise skip, or (variant) take it with no T1 and trail only.
   - Data: daily.
   - Test: compare the expectancy of gated vs ungated trades, and the T1-exit result within the gated subset. This directly tests why T1 lost for us.
2. **No-chase / gap-skip (p275, p295).** M.
   - Rule: cancel the buy-stop if the next open is above the trigger by more than g·ATR, or above the LRP. Variant: allow a fill only if the open is ≤ the LRP.
   - Data: daily OHLC (1h to verify the fill path).
   - Test: skipped trades vs taken trades; net effect on expectancy and trade count.
3. **Two-part scale-out (p265, p302).** M.
   - Rule: 50% exits at T1 (only when T1 ≥ 1R); 50% trails (rule 5 or 6); part 2 goes to BE when T1 fills.
   - Data: daily.
   - Test: vs 100% trail, vs 100% T1, vs a fixed 2R target.
4. **Part 1 BE trigger (p311, p336).** M/A.
   - Rule variants: move part 1 to BE (a) when the high exceeds entry + 1R; (b) on the first new closing high after entry; (c) when price is within 0.25R of T1.
   - Test: vs never moving to BE. Watch for p387's "too early" effect, i.e. an increase in BE scratches that later would have hit T1.
5. **Trail behind with-trend TTF bars (p350, p433).** M.
   - Rule: after each daily bar that closes in the top 1/3 of its range **and** at a new post-entry high, raise the stop to that bar's low − buffer.
   - Data: daily.
   - Test: vs swing-low trail (rule 6), ATR chandelier, and our existing trail.
6. **Trail beyond confirmed swing lows (p311, p314, p318).** M.
   - Rule: the stop sits below the most recent confirmed 2-bar daily swing low (confirmed only after its 2 right bars). Variant: use 1h swing lows only until part 1 exits (the "transition" idea, p303–304), then daily.
   - Data: daily (1h for the variant).
7. **Time-based scratch (p302–303).** M.
   - Rule: exit at close of bar N (N = 2…5) if MFE < k·R (k = 0.5, 1) or if the close is ≤ entry.
   - Data: daily.
   - Test: scratched trades' counterfactual outcomes vs the saved losses; net R.
8. **Follow-through failure scratch (p408, p422).** M/A.
   - Rule: if the entry day (or the next day) closes below the trigger level, i.e. "fails to hold beyond the LWP", exit at that close (or next open). Variant on 1h: exit if within the first H hours price returns below the trigger and makes a lower low.
   - Data: daily, 1h.
9. **R:R tighten near target (p312–313, p387).** M.
   - Rule: when (target − close) < 0.25·(close − stop), move the stop to the prior bar's low, or take profit at the close.
   - Data: daily.
   - Test: vs leaving the stop unchanged.
10. **Remove T2 in trends; T1 = T2 in ranges (p316–317).** A (needs a regime label).
    - Rule: if the weekly trend is up and the daily efficiency ratio > threshold, part 2 has no target and trails. If the regime is "range", part 2 exits at T1 as well.
    - Data: weekly, daily.
    - Test: vs a single policy for all regimes.
11. **T2 = just below the next weekly S/R; no T2 at 52-week highs or ATH (p265–266, p269).** M.
    - Rule: T2 = the next weekly confirmed swing high above T1, minus a buffer. If none exists within the lookback, trail only.
    - Data: weekly, daily.
    - Test: vs pure trail for part 2.
12. **Trap-pullback filter (p242, p250, p496).** M.
    - Rule: in a daily uptrend, require that the pullback's low **undercut the prior confirmed swing low** (or, for a CPB, the 3rd leg undercut the 1st leg's low) and then a close back above it before the buy-stop is armed.
    - Data: daily.
    - Test: trap pullbacks vs non-trap pullbacks vs random. This is distinct from depth alone.
13. **Weakening-trend selection (p250).** A.
    - Rule: if the last extension's projection < the prior one, or the last pullback depth > the prior one ("weakening"), take only CPB/trap PBs and skip simple PBs. In "steady" trends take both.
    - Data: daily swings.
14. **Over-extension → skip the first PB (p241–242).** M.
    - Rule: if at the swing high the close was > x·ATR above the 20 EMA, skip the first single-leg PB and wait for a multi-leg (CPB) pullback.
    - Data: daily.
15. **Max two full losses per setup area (p309).** M.
    - Rule: per stock, after 2 stop-outs (or −2R cumulative) in the same pullback/zone (same swing structure), no further entries until a new swing high forms.
    - Data: daily.
    - Test: re-entries after 0, 1 and 2 losses, plus their expectancy.
16. **Re-entry after a scratch on a fresh trigger (p309).** M.
    - Rule: after a scratch, re-arm the buy-stop above the next bar's high if the setup still holds (pullback low not broken).
    - Test: the expectancy of re-entries.
17. **Counter-trend short filter (p250–251).** A.
    - Rule: allow a short against a weekly/daily uptrend only after a pullback deeper than the prior pullbacks with a strong down-swing, followed by a **lower-momentum retest of the high that fails (BOF)**. Otherwise allow shorts only with-trend in downtrends.
    - Data: daily, weekly.
    - Test: counter-trend vs with-trend short expectancy.
18. **Event blackout (p463, p470, p476).** M.
    - Rule: no new entries within D trading days before earnings (and optionally RBI policy, Budget). Optionally exit or tighten open trades the day before.
    - Data: earnings calendar plus daily.
    - Test: entries just before events vs others; held-through-event P&L distribution.
19. **Gap-day 1h delay (p469, p475).** M.
    - Rule: on days that gap more than g·ATR, no 1h entry in the first hour; arm only after the first hourly bar closes in the trade direction.
    - Data: 1h.
20. **1h spring/upthrust trigger (p288, p617).** M.
    - Rule: with a daily setup active (price in the pullback zone above the stop), a 1h bar whose low pierces the pullback low or support and that closes back above it arms a buy-stop above that 1h bar's high. Stop below the spike.
    - Data: daily plus 1h.
    - Test: vs the daily buy-stop over the prior bar's high. Measure R achieved (tighter stop), win%, and missed-trade rate.
21. **1h stall-limit entry (p284).** M/A.
    - Rule: if ≥3 consecutive 1h (or daily) bars hold above the support zone with no new low, place a limit at the lowest body of the stall. Stop below the zone.
    - Test: fill rate, R, and "fill-then-lose" rate vs the stop entry (the p285 trade-off).
22. **Bracket entry (p294–295).** M.
    - Rule: a working 1h limit near the pullback low **plus** a daily buy-stop at the LWP, one-cancels-other. Only when the LWP is inside the entry zone; otherwise limit only.
    - Data: 1h, daily.
    - Test: vs stop-only and limit-only.
23. **Liquidity/cost filter (p453–454).** M.
    - Rule: trade a stock only if its median daily swing (extension length) ≥ m × round-trip cost (brokerage + STT + slippage), and its median turnover is above a floor.
    - Test: expectancy net of costs by swing/cost bucket. Also check whether 1h or 15m entries still clear costs.
24. **Weekly watchlist of the smoothest trends (p475).** M/A.
    - Rule: each weekend, rank stocks that are trending (weekly HH/HL or price > rising 30-week MA) and above minimum volume by smoothness (efficiency ratio or R² over 3–6 months). Keep the top 20–30 for next week's signals.
    - Test: signals inside vs outside the list.
25. **Portfolio risk cap (p486–487).** M.
    - Rule: 0.5% per trade; total open risk ≤ 3% counting only positions with stop below entry; ≤ 1 (or 2) positions per sector, or a correlation-weighted cap.
    - Test: portfolio simulation for drawdown, CAGR and trade skips vs uncapped.
26. **Weekly drawdown circuit breaker (p481–484, p487).** M.
    - Rule: no new entries for the rest of the week once equity falls 2% (variant 3%) below the week's running equity high. Business stop at a 20% drawdown from peak.
    - Test: in the portfolio sim, whether it reduces drawdown without hurting return. It may act as an unintended regime filter, so evaluate out of sample.
27. **Size step-down after two session stops (p481, p485).** M.
    - Rule: after two weekly stops without a new equity high in between, halve the risk per trade until a new equity high.
    - Test: portfolio sim.

## 4. Process requirements for production

**Risk limits (enforced automatically, with a "Risk Manager" veto; p539, p602):**
- Every order must carry an attached stop (and target where applicable) at submission. No naked positions, ever (p482, p492, p539).
- Risk per trade ≤ 1% of equity (0.5% per part), sized off the stop distance (p481–482). If capital can't support that at the minimum lot (e.g. F&O lot sizes), don't trade that instrument; don't raise the risk (p487).
- Total open risk ≤ 3%, with BE-or-better positions at zero; a correlation/sector cap (p486–487).
- R:R gate: reject entries with < 1:1 reward to T1 (p280, p282). Never chase beyond the entry zone (p275, p295).
- Weekly timeout at 2% and weekly stop at 3%, trailing the week's equity high. Hard limits are 3% and 5% (p483–484, p487).
- Business stop at 20% drawdown (max 30%), then automatic fallback to paper mode (p481, p484). Live minimum-size stage kill switch at a 10% loss (p596).
- Risk amounts recomputed weekly from last week's closing equity (p481, p484, p566).
- Event blackout and pre-event handling for earnings and scheduled macro events (p463, p470, p476).
- **F&O shorts: track expiry and rollover dates; check "no rollover due" before each session** (p547). Treat a rollover as a planned action, never a surprise.

**Contingency handling (p490–492, p561–563):**
- Connectivity or broker failure: a documented flatten path (backup route, broker web, broker phone). Broker emergency contacts on the front page of the runbook, no passwords (p533, p544).
- Drawdown limit hit: exit/flatten as the plan specifies and block new entries (p492).
- Wrong order or fill: auto-detect mismatches (size, side, price vs plan). Cancel if unfilled; if filled, exit or protect. Log it; raise it with the broker if it was their error (p491).
- Rule violation (any manual override that breaks the plan): block new trades, close what should be closed, log it (p492).
- Duplicate-fill protection: one-cancels-other between bracket legs; check for double position size (p295, p348).
- After a fill, confirm full vs partial. After an exit, confirm flat (p321–322).
- Clock sync and order-config checks (instrument, size, order types) before the session (p547).

**Daily reconciliation and admin (p558):**
- Export broker transactions and reconcile them with the app's trade log daily.
- P&L review; a green/red calendar of daily results.

**Journaling fields per trade (p321, p495–496, p558–560, p572):**
- Timestamped signal, order and fill events.
- Stock, side, setup type (TST/BOF/BPB/PB/CPB or our label), regime/environment label (trending/ranging × volatile/non-volatile, p574), weekday.
- Planned entry, stop, T1, T2, LWP, LRP, R:R to T1, position size, risk in money and %.
- Actual fill (slippage vs plan), each part's exit price and reason: target, trail, BE, time-scratch, follow-through scratch, event exit, manual.
- MFE/MAE in R; hindsight-ideal entry, stop and exit; result in R and money, net of costs.
- Re-entry flag and how many losses already occurred in this setup area (p309).
- Missed or skipped signals with their reason (gap-skip, R:R gate, risk cap, blackout) and a hypothetical outcome (p295, p559–560).
- Free-text notes with codes AN / TR / MK / LL / PERS / ? (p572).
- Rule-violation and contingency events.

**Review metrics and cadence (p495–497, p537, p566–568):**
- Win% and WLSR (average win / average loss), plus expectancy in R. Report both including and **excluding breakeven trades**, since his targets exclude BE (p495).
- Breakdown by setup, stock or sector, long/short, regime and weekday. Use samples of ≥ 20 trades and roll small periods forward (p496).
- Compare against our historical or backtest averages; alert when win% or WLSR drift (p496).
- Weekly: stats for all trades and subsets, review notes, replay key trades, set next week's process goal(s), confirm next week's risk amounts (p566).
- Monthly: stats, journal study, **trading-plan review** (p529–530, p567).
- Twice-yearly or annual: goals review (p567).
- Plan-review question to answer every month with evidence: do we really know the plan has positive expectancy, and how? (p568).
- Optional higher-level consistency target: share of positive weeks and months (adapted from 70/90/100, which needs 5 trades/day; p497).

**Promotion gates (p592–597):**
- Backtest plus hindsight study, then paper trading until **4 consecutive profitable blocks of ≥20 trades**.
- Then live at minimum size with the 10% kill switch.
- Then each size increase is proven in paper for one block, followed by 4 profitable live blocks.
- Increases are gradual. Step down after two weekly stops without a new equity high (p481, p485).
- When performance degrades: **reduce size first** (p599), then **simplify** to the best-performing setups and regimes using the stats (p599–600).

**App features implied:**
- Bar-by-bar replay with re-simulation of alternative management (p585–588).
- A "hindsight-perfect" comparison for each trade (p558–560).
- A plan document with a next-review date and current restrictions, e.g. "with-trend only this month" (p533, p538).
- Process-goal tracking, i.e. rules followed vs outcome (p507–508).

## 5. Gaps / discretionary parts

- **Bar-by-bar sentiment reading** (candle classification in context, "does it support my premise?"; p554) drives most scratches, BE moves and target changes in the examples. Rules 7–9 are only crude proxies, and YTC's real edge claim rests on this read (p301).
- **LWP placement in grinding action** (p394–395) and **stall recognition** ("you'll know it when you see it", p284) are subjective. We approximate them with "break of the prior bar's high" and "≥3 bars holding the zone".
- **Environment classification** (steady vs weakening trend, trending vs ranging, volatile or not) is judgement-based. We need a quantitative proxy (projection/depth changes, efficiency ratio), and the setup table (p250) only holds as well as that proxy does.
- **Whether to extend T2 or trail** is "your feel for price action" (p266, p316). Rule 10 is an approximation.
- **When to wait for a CPB rather than take a PB** is largely "feel for the market flow" (p239).
- **BOF bar count** has no fixed rule (p229). **Gaps and session opens** have no fixed rules by design (p469–470, p475).
- **Stock selection** is explicitly not his expertise (p475): no definitive method, only a scan-and-watchlist suggestion.
- **His stats targets** (65% win, WLSR 1.5, 70/90/100) come from intraday discretionary trading with many trades per day and active scratching (p495–497). They are not benchmarks for a daily swing system.
- **Most charts don't extract**: the worked trades (p325–449), the stocks/ETFs examples (p471–474), the replay example (p586–587) and the pattern-variation figure (p578). Only the text descriptions are available.
- **Psychology and fatigue material** (p500–522) applies to a human operator, e.g. one doing manual overrides or monitoring live. It doesn't apply to the system itself, except that manual interventions should be logged and treated as rule violations unless they are part of the plan.
- **Daily-swing specifics** are thin: one weekly/daily/1h stock example with figures only (p471–472) and the multi-position risk notes (p486–487). Everything else is intraday futures/forex. The 1% / 3% / 20% numbers are his intraday plan, adapted by redefining "session" as a week, which he suggests on p487.
- **The book offers no backtest evidence** for any rule. The management claims (active > passive) are explicitly about his psychology, not proven optimality (p301). For a systematic app, each rule above has to earn its place in our own tests.
